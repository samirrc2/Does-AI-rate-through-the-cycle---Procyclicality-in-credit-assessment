Reviewer #1, Concern #5

Reviewer comment:

5. The through-the-cycle prompting experiment is interesting, but the interpretation that
the instruction "anchors rather than de-cyclifies" the models requires stronger validation.
The conclusion mainly relies on the reduction in the verbalized one-year PD slope. Since
LLM-generated probabilities may themselves be poorly calibrated, additional calibration
analysis or alternative measures of point-in-time risk sensitivity would strengthen this
conclusion.

Author response:

  - We agree that the anchoring interpretation should not rest on verbalised PDs. Section 4.3
    now supports it with three measures.
  - The rating-token expected-notch slope, which never reads a stated PD, falls under the TTC
    instruction for every model that exposes token log-probabilities (Table 4). The rating
    anchoring therefore holds in the model's full rating distribution, not only in its stated
    rating.
  - PD retention on the log-odds scale lies below 0.75 for every model, ranging from 0.587 to
    −0.106 (Appendix A).
  - The “be stable” control holds the elicitation fixed and varies only the instruction. It
    preserves far more PD movement than the TTC instruction.
  - We agree that verbalised PDs need not be calibrated. The log-odds scale reduces dependence
    on PD-level differences between models but is not a calibration test, and the manuscript
    now says so. The anchoring result uses changes in PD, not PD levels.

Author action:

  - Table 4 reports expected-notch slopes under both the terse and TTC framings.
  - Appendix A, “Formal contrasts and sensitivity,” adds bootstrap intervals for PD retention
    and the stable-versus-TTC contrasts, and reports “be stable” PD retention.
  - Section 4.3 sets out the three measures and the calibration limitation.

Reviewer #1, Concern #7

Reviewer comment:

7. The experimental design uses two runs per condition at temperature zero. Although the
authors report a within-cell noise analysis, LLM outputs may still vary across repeated API
calls and model updates. Increasing the number of repeated runs and reporting the stability
of the main findings across repetitions would provide stronger evidence regarding the
robustness of the reported effects.

Author response:

  - We agree and collected three further seeds for the primary condition. Four models now have
    five replicates per cell. The other three retain two because their extra-seed captures
    exceeded the 2% error-rate gate set in the pre-registration (Section 5), which is SHA-256
    frozen and re-verified on every reproduction run.
  - The gate operates on whole captures, not on individual observations: freeze.py refuses an
    entire file whose error rate exceeds the threshold and has no mechanism for discarding rows,
    so no observation was dropped for being inconvenient. The three rejected extra-seed captures
    failed for distinct infrastructure reasons: Gemini Flash-Lite returned 400 INVALID_ARGUMENT on
    every call, GPT-6 Astra hit 429 rate limits, and Gemini Pro exhausted its credit quota with 402.
  - For completeness, the gate refused a fourth capture outside the seed extension: a real-firm
    GPT-6 Astra through-the-cycle run at a 10.6% error rate. It was not re-run and feeds no
    reported result, because the real-firm arm reports the terse-framing slope and the
    through-the-cycle estimates come from the frontier and full arms, all of which froze cleanly.
    All four refusals are listed with their error rates and causes in
    results/revision_extras.json.
  - Re-measurement 2.5 months later separates replication noise from version drift. Coefficient
    drift lies within sampling uncertainty, with Δβ from −0.037 to +0.002. We therefore treat
    the coefficients as properties of the served endpoint at the collection epoch.

Author action:

  - Table 4 adds a Noise SD column, and Table 1 adds a Δβ column.
  - Appendix A, “Replication and model drift” (new), reports the drift analysis.
